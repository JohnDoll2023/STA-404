gap2007 <- gapminder %>%
  filter(year == 2007) %>%
  select(c(continent, gdpPercap, lifeExp))

ggplot(data = gap2007, aes(x=gdpPercap, y=lifeExp)) +
  geom_point(aes(color=continent)) +
  geom_smooth(method="loess", se=FALSE)  +
  geom_smooth(aes(color=continent), method="loess", se=FALSE, span = 1) +
  labs(x = "GDP Per Capita", y = "Life Expectancy") +
  annotate("text", x = 20000, y = 72, label = "Global Curve")

The inclusion of the curves does improve the story because you can get a better look at how the data is moving across the graph for each continent and for the world as a whole. Additionally, this helps us compare between continents to see how one continent stacks up against another. Looking at the overall world curve, we can see that the in general, an increase in GDP Per Capita relates to an increase in Life Expectancy.
